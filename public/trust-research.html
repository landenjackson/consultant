Consumer AI Trust Boundaries: Statistical Modeling & The Human Gate

Empirical findings from FSU Qualtrics & SPSS research isolating user privacy friction, data boundaries, and long-term retention dynamics.
1. The Privacy vs. Confidence Gap (p < .001)

A Paired Samples T-Test confirmed that automated data storage is perceived as a significant privacy risk (p < .001). While consumers utilize AI for basic tasks, sensitive decision-making triggers immediate hesitation without clear oversight.

Statistical Significance: p < .001 (Confidence Threshold Exceeded)
2. Data Security & Long-Term Retention Correlation (r = 0.38)

Pearson Correlation identified a direct positive relationship (r = 0.38, p < .01) between perceived data sovereignty and weekly active retention. Users stay when they know their data is private and not exposed to public model training.

Retention Correlation: r = 0.38 (p < .01)
3. The 4.2x Trust Advantage of Human-in-the-Loop

Completely autonomous AI outputs incur a significant trust penalty. Pairing high-speed computational drafting with human operator verification eliminates autonomy risk and restores executive confidence.

Trust Multiplier: 4.2x Higher Conversion with Human Validation
